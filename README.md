# aqn-control

PESOSE: Track 1: Scoping a Secure and Sustainable Open-Source Ecosystem for the Arkansas Quantum Network in Healthcare and Government Infrastructure
PROJECT SUMMARY

Project Overview: 
This Track 1 project scopes and plans a secure, sustainable, and resilient Open-Source Ecosystem (OSE) centered on the Arkansas Quantum Network (AQN) software control protocols as its foundational research product. Framed as an experimental, to-be-created physical network, the AQN will connect the University of Arkansas at Little Rock (UA Little Rock) and the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences (UAMS) in its initial phase, with planning for a subsequent extension to the University of Arkansas at Pine Bluff (UAPB). The network will leverage the high-capacity, existing fiber optic backbone of the Arkansas Research and Education Optical Network (ARE-ON) by utilizing dedicated dark fiber strands. The to-be-created physical network serves as a living laboratory to study the co-existence and software orchestration of Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) and Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) over a single network of entangled qubits. The proposed project includes full participation in the mandatory “I-Corps for PESOSE” (specifically the “I-Corps for Quantum+X”) experiential training.

Intellectual Merit: 
The intellectual merit of the project lies in defining open-source API standards and orchestration software that connect classical High-Performance Computing (HPC) environments with quantum hardware stacks. This includes scoping advanced control plane security primitives, specifically entanglement-verification, timing jitter mitigation, and fidelity-checking protocols, to secure the classical orchestration layer against hostile routing manipulation or timing delay exploits. Furthermore the project applies evidence-based education models to translate abstract quantum mechanics into structured academic curricula, researching how 'scaffolding' mathematical concepts reduces historical trepidation and increases cybersecurity student retention in the quantum-safe transition.

Broader Impacts: 
The broader impacts focus on national security, regional critical infrastructure resilience, and addressing the massive 59% global workforce reskilling gap expected by 2030. The establishment of a robust, open-source quantum-safe stack provides regional government agencies and healthcare centers (UAMS medical informatics) with a roadmap to achieve 'cryptographic agility' to defend sensitive patient, genomic, and municipal records against the looming 'Quantum Threat'. To expand participation, the project embeds microcredentials and stackable digital badges within regional STEM educational frameworks, ensuring a demographically and geographically diverse talent pipeline.
